LRN · Case study 05

Build a CRM-integrated platform for provisioning and license management

0→1PlatformAutomationRollout strategy
The problem

The mature LRN platform was experiencing overuse and revenue leakage from existing provisioning and licensing processes. We needed to integrate CRM with the platform to automate provisioning, entitlement, and license management while limiting disruption to existing customers.

My product decisions

I built the CRM-integrated provisioning, entitlement, and license management capability as an internal platform capability, working across Product, Engineering, Sales, Revenue Operations, Delivery, and Support.

Because this was a mature platform with existing customers, I treated the rollout as part of the product strategy—not simply a deployment exercise. I designed a gradual transition: new customers would move to the automated process immediately, while existing customers would transition at renewal.

I worked with Delivery and Support to plan the transition around existing customer needs, allowing the organization to introduce the new capability while minimizing disruption to customers still using the existing process.

PRODUCT REASONING
Mature platformPROBLEM
Automation needINSIGHT
CRM-integrated capabilityDECISION
Staged transitionDIRECTION
Outcome
Automated provisioning, entitlement, and license management for new customers, while establishing a controlled path to transition existing customers at renewal.
Product lesson
When changing a mature platform, the product strategy includes not only what you build, but how you transition customers to it.

Clickatell · Case study 10

Turn AI into part of the customer journey

AICustomer experienceJourney designProduct integration
The problem

Clickatell had a separate AI chatbot capability. The opportunity was to make AI part of the broader customer engagement journey rather than leave it as a standalone chatbot.

My product decisions

I integrated the separate Clickatell AI chatbot as a full-fledged agent within the existing customer engagement journey.

The journey could move from customer → AI, AI → human when needed, human → AI when needed or triggered, and ultimately human or AI → end.

The product decision was to make AI part of one connected customer journey rather than create another standalone destination.

PRODUCT REASONING
